My yearly tradition is back. Here are the Mac apps I've used every day this year. Since AI came along I actually use far fewer apps, just these 25. Let me know which ones you use too, and tell me your own favorite apps of 2026.

I'll go in whatever order they come to mind, not the order in the image above.

1. [**Mole**](https://mole.fit/): I use it almost every day to clean out junk and to check system status from the menu bar. I also use its uninstaller, keep-awake and fan tools daily, and I work on it every day too.
2. [**Claude Code**](https://www.claude.com/product/claude-code): These days I mostly use the CLI, and it's running nearly all the time. I default to Opus 5.5 on high, and switch to Fable 5.2 on medium for the hard stuff. I no longer use Codex, and I don't use any multi-agent tools either. I like coding that stays simple and under my control.
3. [**Kaku**](https://kaku.fun/): I like a terminal that's simple, good-looking out of the box, and light. I run Claude in several tabs on it every day, with a black-background TUI and the [JetBrains Mono](https://www.jetbrains.com/lp/mono/) font, which is still my favorite coding font three years running.
4. [**ego lite**](https://lite.ego.app/): The browser that surprised me most this year, simple, pretty and easy to use. Before this I mostly used [Helium Browser](https://helium.computer/), and if you rarely let AI drive your browser, I'd still recommend Helium, it's light, fast and clean. What I like about ego is that it gives the agent its own separate window, so it never gets in the way of my own browsing.
5. [**1Password**](https://1password.com/): This year I gradually moved all my passwords here. It works across platforms and the overall quality is good. Mostly I wanted some peace of mind in an age when AI can crack just about anything. The price is reasonable, and once I got used to it, I've found it really pleasant to use.
6. [**MiaoYan**](https://miaoyan.app/): A light, native Markdown notes app that looks good and works well by default. Since the iPhone version came out, I open it almost every day to jot things down. The [Weekly](https://weekly.tw93.fun/) newsletter you read each week is edited in the Mac app, and everything else I write starts as a draft in it too.
7. [**Grok Bot**](https://x.ai/bot): Maybe not everyone will like this one, but it has helped me a lot with running and growing my products: answering questions, handling refunds, logging issues to Notion, plus my daily investment report, X digest and Weekly digest. All of that comes from this, working as my personal agent.
8. [**ScreenFlow**](https://www.telestream.net/screenflow/): My default app for recording the screen and editing video. It's durable, simple and powerful, and I mostly use it to record product demos and speed up videos.
9. [**Apple Music**](https://www.apple.com/apple-music/): I've tried a lot of music players, and Apple's own is still my favorite. The subscription is cheap, the audio quality is great, and the music I listen to is all there. I like that it's clean and works across my devices. I don't care for flashy extras, and this is just right.
10. [**Pake**](https://pake.run/): A tool that makes it easy to turn a web page into a desktop app. I've packaged WeChat Read and a music player for Li Zhi, a Chinese folk singer I love, into Mac apps, then I open them with Raycast hotkeys. It's really handy, and you can customize a lot to your own taste.
11. [**Shottr**](https://shottr.cc/): If I had to recommend one screenshot tool that's the best and simplest, it's still this one. I think it's only about 4 dollars, and it seems you can use it without paying. It's light, and I love it for window screenshots and scrolling screenshots. I've used it for years.
12. [**Things**](https://culturedcode.com/things/): A very clean to-do app. Because it's so simple, it actually helps me stay focused.
13. [**Parallels**](https://www.parallels.com/): Opinions on this one may be mixed, but I still think it's the best Windows virtual machine. Maybe it's my M5 Pro, but running Windows on it doesn't feel like a VM at all, it's really smooth. It helped me a lot while building Windows apps.
14. [**Bob**](https://bobtranslate.com/): A translator I reach for all the time. It's not expensive either. I hooked it up to my own AI key and set a hotkey, so it's there whenever I need it. Very nice.
15. [**Input Source Pro**](https://inputsource.pro/): It switches your input method for you, so where you write it defaults to Chinese, and where you code it defaults to English. That's one less shortcut to press, and it works quietly in the background.
16. [**NetNewsWire**](https://netnewswire.com/): A light RSS reader that works well by default, with an iPhone version too. I recommend my own [MiaoYan theme](https://github.com/tw93/MiaoYan-NetNewsWire-Theme), it's very comfortable. In the AI era I think we still need to keep reading, and this is a great tool for it.
17. [**Sketch**](https://www.sketch.com/): Ha, okay, I admit it, all my design work, logos and drafts are still done in Sketch. Maybe it's because it's a native app and I'm stubborn about that. It does everything I need and feels comfortable. Maybe I'm just used to it, so I don't pay much attention to the newer tools.
18. [**Top Calendar**](https://guojiubo.github.io/): The best calendar for the menu bar. You can replace the system clock in the menu bar with it, and a click shows your calendar and upcoming events. It also just quietly works.
19. [**AyuGram**](https://github.com/AyuGram/AyuGramDesktop): A clean, easy, open-source Telegram client. It removes a lot of the premium upsells and ads from the official app, and has tons of customization options, though the defaults are really all you need. I open it once or twice a day to catch up on messages.
20. [**Helium**](https://helium.computer/): I thought I liked vertical tabs, but what I actually wanted was a minimal UI and maximum working space. Its compact mode puts the address bar and tabs on the same row, clean and tight. Fully open source, privacy first, unbiased ad blocking, no bloat.
21. [**Raycast**](https://www.raycast.com/): The only app on this list that feels a bit heavy to me, yet I still use it every day. On another computer I run [Tinycast](https://tinycast.dev/) alongside it for comparison. I use Raycast's clipboard history, app hotkeys and file search almost every day. The catch is the first setup, you have to turn off a lot of features I consider unnecessary before it feels good. Once Tinycast matures, I might move my main machine over to it.
22. [**uPic**](https://github.com/gee1k/uPic): The tool I've used for years to upload images to my CDN. Why this one? Again it's my soft spot for native, light apps. Once it's bound to a hotkey, I think it's great.
23. [**Command X**](https://sindresorhus.com/command-x): A tiny tool that lets your Mac cut and paste files in Finder. That's all it does, haha.
24. [**VS Code**](https://code.visualstudio.com/): I used it every day before AI, and I still use it often, mostly for reading diffs, tidying up files and reading READMEs. But I suspect I'll stop using it next year and find something lighter and native.
25. [**MindNode**](https://www.mindnode.com/): A tool that draws really nice mind maps. I've used it since I started working, for brainstorming and thinking things through quickly.
